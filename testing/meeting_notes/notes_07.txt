Lena Kovacs: I'll follow up with Clara directly after this meeting. Feature flag requests shouldn't sit for more than a couple of days. Chloe, your turn. What came out of the user research?
Chloe Barnes: I ran eight in-depth interviews last week, all with power users — people who log in more than three times a week. The insight that surprised me most was about recommendation relevance. Users don't want recommendations based purely on historical behaviour — what they bought last month. They want recommendations that are contextual to what they're trying to do right now, in this session. One user put it well: she said it's the difference between a good search result and a good ad. The ad knows your history; the search result knows your intent.
Tariq Salim: That's a significant design implication. Right now the model Nina and I are building is behaviour-based — it uses historical interaction data to predict what a user might want next. Adding session-level context would require real-time signals we're not currently capturing.
Chloe Barnes: I thought that might be the case. The question is whether it's worth adding to the first version or whether we ship the behaviour-based model first and iterate.
Lena Kovacs: My instinct is to ship the simpler model first and not delay the launch. But Chloe, has Nina's team seen this research?
Chloe Barnes: No — I finished writing it up yesterday. I wasn't sure who to send it to.
Tariq Salim: Send it to me and I'll make sure Nina gets it. It's directly relevant to how the feature store is structured.
Victor Nguyen: Before we move on — from a revenue perspective, the recommendation engine's commercial value depends entirely on what it's recommending.
